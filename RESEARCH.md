# Balloon Bonkers: audience and creative research

The research step behind every post. Read this before writing. Updated 2026-10-25.
Sources are listed at the bottom; numbers from marketing blogs are their own estimates.

## 0. CURRENT PHASE: unlock players under 16 (checked 2026-10-25)

Balloon Bonkers is public but only for **ages 16+ and trusted friends**. To reach players
under 16 (Roblox Select 9-15 and Kids 5-8), Roblox needs **250 unique plays from highly engaged,
age-checked players 16+ within a 60-day window**. Status on 25 Oct: **3 of 250**. Every other
step is done (rating, ID, 2FA, publishing fee; fee refunded 19 Jan 2027).
"Highly engaged" is judged on account age, play history and platform spend, so brand-new or
alt accounts don't help.

What this means for marketing until the 250 are reached:
- **Target players 16+**, not young kids. A kid who clicks now can't join, which wastes the
  post and can annoy them.
- Pitch it as a **party game for friends and chaos fans**: older teens, young adults,
  streamers, and other Roblox developers.
- Best channels for 16+ Roblox players: Roblox DevForum (developers play each other's games),
  r/roblox and r/robloxgamedev, Roblox Discord communities, friends and family with
  age-checked accounts, and TikTok/Shorts with humour that works for older teens.
- Spooky Night content still works, but frame it for 16+.
- Track the counter weekly: Creator Hub → Balloon Bonkers → Manage audience.
- Day-1 retention was 1.82% on a tiny sample (7-day average, 25 Oct). Watch it once real
  players arrive: if it stays low, the first minute of the game needs work.

## 1. Who plays (consumer insight)

- Roblox had about 132 million daily players in Q1 2026. Among age-checked players:
  roughly 36% under 13, 38% aged 13-17, 26% adults (Roblox Q1 2026 shareholder letter).
  So most players are kids and teens, and growth is fastest outside the US and Canada.
- Instagram and Facebook are 13+, and parents are there. TikTok and YouTube Shorts are where
  kids and teens actually find new Roblox games.
- What they want from a game like ours, in their words: *funny moments with friends*,
  *being the one who wins*, *something to show off*, *quick rounds*.
- What makes them leave: confusion in the first minute, waiting, losing without understanding why.

## 2. Why people stop scrolling (behavioural insight)

Use these, ethically, in every post:

| Driver | What it means | Balloon Bonkers example |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise | Something unexpected in the first second | The whale swallows the balloon mid-hit |
| Humour / schadenfreude | Watching someone else fail | GOTCHA: a smash the next player has no chance against |
| Tension | "Will they make it?" | Last heart, balloon falling, dive save at the last frame |
| Competence / challenge | "I could do that" or "could I?" | "Only 1 in 10 players can save this" (only if true) or "could you save this?" |
| Social | Playing with friends | "Tag the friend you'd GOTCHA" / "bring a friend" |
| Collection / identity | Owning something cool | A balloon skin reveal |

Never use: fake scarcity, "buy now" pressure, "free Robux", fake numbers, or anything that
pushes kids to spend. Children's advertising rules (CARU) ban manipulative tactics aimed at
getting kids to buy, and require that real-money purchases are clear. Roblox's own discovery
rules also penalise bait titles and leading with Robux or giveaways.

## 3. Creative strategy: the hook

What works for Roblox short videos (TikTok / YouTube Shorts):
- **The first 3 seconds must work without sound and without context.** Start mid-action,
  show the most surprising frame first, and put the stakes on screen in a few words
  ("LAST HEART", "1 HIT LEFT").
- **No logo, no intro, no trailer.** They underperform.
- **Structure:** hook → moment → payoff → call to action.
- **Length:** 15-30 seconds for TikTok, 20-45 for Shorts; never over 60.
- **Real reaction helps:** a voice or a text reaction ("NOOO") beats silence.
- **Call to action:** "Search BALLOON BONKERS on Roblox", on screen and in the caption.

Clip formats for Balloon Bonkers, in priority order:
1. **GOTCHA fails:** a smash that makes the next player miss.
2. **Clutch saves:** last-second dive save on the last heart.
3. **Hazard chaos:** whale, shark, robo-vacuum or cat paw ruining a rally.
4. **Bandit revenge:** a knocked-out player hitting the player who knocked them out.
5. **POV skits:** "POV: your best friend smashes it at you."
6. **Update teasers:** new map, balloon or event, shown in the first second.

Titles: game name + moment + hook word, e.g. "Balloon Bonkers: the dive save nobody expected".

## 4. Channels, in order of player impact

1. **TikTok + YouTube Shorts** (through Metricool): 3-5 clips a week. The same clip goes to
   both, since the audiences are different. Expect slow growth: blogs report the first big
   clip often comes after 20-40 uploads, so consistency beats perfection.
2. **Roblox itself**: recommendations are ranked on what players do, not on followers:
   play-through rate from the home page, first-play bounces (<60 s and 61-180 s),
   play days per user over day 1, days 2-7 and days 8-28, playtime per user, and co-play
   with friends. Players arriving from social media help the game get *considered*, but
   only home-page players count in the *ranking*. Marketing a game that doesn't keep
   players wastes the effort, so the weekly report must check retention.
3. **Instagram + Facebook** (our poster): screenshots, art, news. Small reach for now;
   good for parents and for looking active.
4. **Communities**: DevForum feedback section, r/robloxgamedev. Check each venue's rules first.
5. **Small creators**: pick by the views on their last 10-20 videos, not by followers.
   Any paid or gifted promotion must be labelled as an ad.

## 5. How we measure (data analyst)

Every Monday:
- Posting log: date, platform, format, hook, views after 2 days, average watch time.
- Roblox Creator Dashboard: daily players, new players, day-1 and day-7 retention,
  and Home Recommendations (Analytics → Acquisition), on posting days vs the same weekday before.
- Test, don't guess: for a good moment, make 2-3 versions with different hooks and keep the winner.
- If views go up but players don't stay, the problem is the game's first minute,
  not the marketing. Report that to Brian.

## 6. What works for game Shorts right now (web research, 26 Oct 2026)

Strongest evidence is what the platforms themselves say they measure. Indie case studies
are self-reported. No verified Roblox clip-level case study was found.

What we copy:
1. **Finishing and rewatching** is TikTok's strongest signal; followers don't matter directly (TikTok, official).
   → 18-25 s, no dead frames, end quickly. Payoff in the last 3 s so people watch to the end.
2. **Sends** (sharing in DMs) are Instagram's top signal for reaching non-followers (Mosseri, Jan 2025).
   → On-screen text that names a type of friend ("THAT ONE FRIEND WHO ALWAYS MISSES") so people send it.
     We never write "send this / tag a friend" (bait); the text just makes it sendable.
3. **The point in the first 3 s, with text on screen** (TikTok ad data). → Open mid-action, never on a title.
4. **One idea per clip**, then re-edit the winner. The same footage cut faster went from 10k to 7M views (The Matriarch).
   → Make 2-3 cuts of a good moment; keep the one with the best "viewed vs swiped away".
5. **Party games break out on funny emergent moments** (R.E.P.O.), not trailers. → Real chaos, real reactions, game SFX.
6. **Volume and consistency**: one breakout lifts the next videos (DREADMOOR). → Keep 3-5 clips a week, don't give up early.
7. **Game name on screen the whole clip.** TikTok bio links need 1,000 followers and Shorts links aren't clickable,
   so search is the path. → Small "BALLOON BONKERS • Roblox" bar under the gameplay, plus the end card.
8. **Real questions are fine, bait is penalised** (TikTok For You rules). → End captions with a real question,
   e.g. "What hazard should we add next?", then build/credit the best answer.
9. **Never upload a file with another app's watermark** (TikTok and Instagram downrank it). → Always upload our clean mp4.
10. **Measure Shorts by engaged views and "viewed vs swiped away"**, not raw views (YouTube changed view counting in Mar 2025).
11. **Roblox share links** (Creator Dashboard) show which platform brings engaged players. → One link per platform (to do).
12. Possible extra channels to test: **Instagram Trial Reels** (shown only to non-followers, good for A/B hooks) and
    **Roblox Moments** (in-app clip feed, 13+ beta, viewers jump straight into the game).

### 6b. Case: Kio, "How Much ROBUX I Make as a Small Roblox Developer" (YouTube, 25 Oct 2026; self-reported)
Horror game ANALOG, released ~6-10 Oct 2026, peak 4.1K CCU, claims ~873K Robux in under 2 weeks.
- **250 engaged players (Kids & Select unlock) in 2.5 days** with a Roblox Ads Manager campaign on the
  **Engagement** objective (~10 ad credits/day, 5.8% CTR). That objective exists exactly for this threshold
  (Roblox docs). Costs Robux/money, so it is Brian's call; we stay free unless he says otherwise.
- After unlock: Plays ads (9-10% CTR), and he kept ads running until Home Recommendation impressions overtook
  sponsored impressions, then stopped. Ads alone bring "cold" players who bounce; social brings warmer ones.
- His viral Short (1.3M views) = one unique mechanic as a question, answered instantly:
  "Must be quiet... but why? Because it hears your mic." → Our equivalent: "Make your friend miss" / "Knocked out? Send a whale".
- An earlier game grew from a "Day 1 of making a ___ game" devlog series (several 0.5-1.7M-view Shorts, 15K Discord in a week).
  → Test a devlog series from Brian's side ("Day N of making a Roblox party game").
- OUR RESULT (27 Oct): Brian already ran an Engagement campaign (63 USD over 28 days, 69 plays, engaged count 2→3)
  and paused it. Kio's result did not repeat for us; no more ad spend unless Brian decides otherwise.
- He uses a game-named TikTok account. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mMfwlQKrHCw

Stop: slow intros or logo stingers, feature-list montages, deleting and reposting Shorts that flop, over-polished trailer cuts.
